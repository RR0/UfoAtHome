3D decor (rr0-scene)

A sky/horizon/starfield backdrop computed from each sighting's real reported time and place — the documented cases of the site's demos, spanning night, dawn, midday and dusk. Astronomically accurate: Sun/Moon/planet positions, the Moon's phase, and a real star catalog (magnitude ≤ 7.5) for the given instant and location.

The sky tests are not cases and hold no recorded shape at all: they are skies set up with the conditions one sight needs, for looking at that sight. A rainbow wants rain, a Sun low enough for the bow to clear the horizon, a gap in the cloud for the light to come through, and an observer facing away from the Sun — all four, or there is nothing to see. The Milky Way and the zodiacal light are the same kind of conjunction and it is worth knowing which conditions they are: no Moon at all, a Sun far enough down, a clear sky, and the plane in question standing high rather than lying on the horizon.

Two buttons sit beside the fullscreen one. The map shows, over real aerial imagery, where the observer stood, which way they faced, and how wide a piece of ground their instrument took in — Socorro is the case it was built for, since Zamora covered eleven hundred metres of road and turned his back on the object to run. The other takes the account's own named moments off: the ticks on the bar, the caption, and the lettered points on the map. Both tags carry show-observer-map here, which only decides that the map starts open; the button is there either way.
